Planning a Gorilla Trek? Before or After the World Cup

July 29, 2026 10 min read Gorilla Trekking

Watching mountain gorillas in their natural habitat is one of Africa's most extraordinary wildlife experiences. Every year, thousands of travellers visit Uganda and Rwanda to trek through ancient rainforests in search of these gentle giants. If you're planning your safari around a major global sporting event like the FIFA World Cup, you may be wondering whether it's better to go before or after the tournament.

The answer depends on your travel goals, budget, preferred level of crowds, and overall itinerary. Both options offer incredible opportunities for gorilla trekking, but each comes with unique advantages. This guide explores the pros and cons of traveling before or after the World Cup to help you make the best decision for your East African adventure.

Why Combine Gorilla Trekking with the World Cup?

For many international travelers, especially those flying long distances, combining a safari with the World Cup creates the ultimate African holiday. Whether you're attending matches in the host country or traveling around the continent, Uganda and Rwanda are easily accessible through major African airline hubs.

A gorilla trekking safari offers the perfect contrast to the excitement of football. After cheering for your favorite team, you can immerse yourself in tranquil rainforests, encounter endangered mountain gorillas, and experience the rich cultures of East Africa.

Gorilla Trekking Before the World Cup

Traveling before the World Cup has several advantages, especially if you prefer a quieter and more relaxed safari.

Advantages

  • Greater availability of gorilla permits
  • Lower travel demand and fewer tourists
  • More relaxed travel schedule
  • Better availability of accommodation
  • Quieter national parks

Best For

  • Travelers seeking peace and solitude
  • Those with flexible schedules
  • Budget-conscious travelers
  • Photographers wanting fewer crowds
  • Travelers wanting to unwind before the tournament

Greater Availability of Gorilla Permits

Gorilla trekking permits are issued in limited numbers every day to protect the endangered mountain gorillas. During periods leading up to major international events, demand may still be relatively moderate, making it easier to secure permits for your preferred dates.

Uganda currently offers more gorilla trekking permits than Rwanda because Bwindi Impenetrable National Park is home to over 20 habituated gorilla families. This provides greater flexibility when planning your safari.

Lower Travel Demand

Many travelers delay their vacations until the World Cup begins. Visiting beforehand often means fewer tourists in national parks, shorter waiting times, and a more peaceful trekking experience. Hotels, safari lodges, and domestic flights may also have better availability before peak travel periods.

Relaxed Travel Schedule

Starting your African holiday with a safari allows you to unwind before the excitement of the tournament. Instead of rushing between destinations, you'll enjoy the forests, wildlife, and scenic landscapes at a comfortable pace.

Gorilla Trekking After the World Cup

Planning your safari after the World Cup is equally rewarding and may suit travellers who prefer ending their holiday with nature and relaxation.

Advantages

  • Perfect way to unwind after the tournament
  • Extend your African holiday
  • Explore more destinations
  • Combine football with nature and wildlife
  • End your trip with relaxation

Best For

  • Travelers wanting to end with nature
  • Those attending World Cup matches
  • Travelers seeking a post-event escape
  • Those wanting to combine multiple destinations

A Perfect Way to Unwind

The World Cup brings excitement, crowds, and busy cities. After the tournament, many travellers appreciate slowing down with a safari in Uganda or Rwanda. Imagine replacing stadium crowds with misty mountain forests, birdsong, and the unforgettable moment of standing just a few meters away from a family of mountain gorillas.

Extend Your African Holiday

If you've already traveled to Africa for the World Cup, adding a gorilla trekking safari allows you to experience another side of the continent.

Many travelers combine football with:

  • Gorilla trekking
  • Chimpanzee trekking
  • Big Five safaris
  • Cultural experiences
  • Relaxation at Lake Kivu or the beaches of Zanzibar

Will Gorilla Trekking Be Crowded During the World Cup?

One concern many travelers have is whether gorilla trekking will become overcrowded during the World Cup. Fortunately, gorilla trekking remains carefully regulated. Only a limited number of visitors are allowed to visit each gorilla family every day. In Uganda and Rwanda, a maximum of eight visitors may trek a habituated gorilla family during each session. This strict conservation policy ensures that every trekking experience remains intimate, regardless of the season.

Uganda vs Rwanda for Gorilla Trekking

Uganda

Uganda is one of the world's leading gorilla trekking destinations, making it an excellent choice for travelers seeking longer safaris and outstanding value for money. Home to nearly half of the world's remaining mountain gorillas, Uganda offers unforgettable primate encounters alongside diverse wildlife, breathtaking scenery, and rich cultural experiences.

  • Bwindi Impenetrable National Park — UNESCO World Heritage Site with over 20 habituated gorilla families across four trekking sectors
  • Mgahinga Gorilla National Park — Quieter gorilla trekking in the Virunga Mountains with golden monkey tracking and volcano hiking
  • Affordable permits — More budget-friendly than Rwanda
  • Combine with wildlife safaris — Queen Elizabeth, Murchison Falls, and Kibale Forest

Rwanda

Rwanda is an excellent destination for travelers seeking a luxury gorilla trekking experience or those with limited time. Its modern infrastructure, short travel distances, and high-end accommodations make it possible to enjoy an unforgettable safari even on a short itinerary.

  • Volcanoes National Park — Premier gorilla trekking destination in the scenic Virunga Mountains
  • Easy access from Kigali — Just 2.5 to 3 hours from Kigali International Airport
  • Luxury lodges — World-class accommodations with stunning volcanic views
  • Excellent road infrastructure — Well-maintained paved roads throughout the country

Best Time to Go Gorilla Trekking

Mountain gorillas can be visited throughout the year. The best trekking conditions occur during the dry seasons:

  • June to September — Drier trails and easier hiking conditions
  • December to February — Another excellent period for trekking

The rainy seasons — from March to May and October to November — bring lush green forests and fewer visitors. Although trails may be muddier, gorilla trekking remains possible and rewarding.

Tips for Planning Around the World Cup

  • Book your gorilla permits several months in advance
  • Reserve accommodation and domestic flights early, especially for luxury lodges
  • Allow extra travel days between the World Cup host country and East Africa
  • Purchase comprehensive travel insurance
  • Ensure you meet all visa and vaccination requirements

Suggested Gorilla Trekking Itineraries

  • 3-Day Gorilla Trekking Safari — Ideal for travelers with limited time
  • 5-Day Gorilla and Wildlife Safari — Combines gorilla trekking with game drives
  • 7-Day Primates Adventure — Includes gorillas, chimpanzees, and cultural experiences
  • 10-Day Uganda or Rwanda Safari — A complete wildlife and primate adventure
  • Cross-Border Uganda and Rwanda Safari — Experience the highlights of both countries

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I trek gorillas before or after the World Cup?

Both options are excellent. Traveling before the World Cup often offers greater permit availability and fewer crowds, while traveling afterward provides a relaxing way to conclude your African holiday.

How far are Uganda and Rwanda from major African cities?

Both countries are well connected through international airports in Kigali and Entebbe, with convenient flights from major African hubs.

Can I combine gorilla trekking with a Big Five safari?

Yes. Uganda allows you to combine gorilla trekking with game drives in Queen Elizabeth National Park, Murchison Falls National Park, and Kidepo Valley National Park. Rwanda offers Big Five safaris in Akagera National Park alongside gorilla trekking in Volcanoes National Park.

How physically demanding is gorilla trekking?

Gorilla trekking requires a moderate level of fitness. Treks can last from one to six hours or more depending on the gorillas' location, but experienced ranger guides tailor groups according to fitness levels.

Final Thoughts

Whether you choose to embark on your gorilla trekking safari before or after the World Cup, you'll experience one of Africa's most unforgettable wildlife adventures. Standing face-to-face with endangered mountain gorillas in the misty forests of Uganda or Rwanda is a life-changing experience that few destinations can match.

If your priority is easier permit availability and a quieter safari, traveling before the tournament may be the better option. If you'd rather celebrate the end of the World Cup with an immersive escape into nature, a post-tournament safari offers the perfect balance of adventure and relaxation.

Whichever you choose, booking early, securing your gorilla permits, and working with an experienced safari operator will ensure a seamless journey and memories that last a lifetime.
