7 DAYS GORILLA AND CHIMPANZEE TREKKING

Primate Safari Gorilla Trekking Chimpanzee Tracking Forest Adventure Nature Photography

The Ultimate Primate Safari in Rwanda

Embark on a 7-day primate odyssey through Rwanda's most spectacular forests, combining two of Africa's most sought-after wildlife experiences. This immersive safari takes you from the misty volcanic slopes of Volcanoes National Park - home to the endangered mountain gorillas - to the ancient rainforests of Nyungwe Forest National Park, where troops of chimpanzees swing through the canopy. Experience close encounters with both of humanity's closest living relatives, trekking through dramatically different ecosystems while learning about primate behavior, conservation efforts, and Rwanda's remarkable commitment to protecting its natural heritage. This expertly crafted itinerary offers the perfect balance of adventure, education, and comfort, with expert guides ensuring unforgettable encounters with Rwanda's most charismatic primates.

Physical Difficulty:
Moderate to Challenging (Both treks involve hiking in forested, sometimes steep terrain)

⚠️ Important Primate Trekking Preparation

Permit Requirements: Gorilla permits ($1,500 per person) must be secured 6-9 months in advance for peak seasons. Chimpanzee permits ($90) should be booked at least 3 months in advance. Age restrictions: gorilla trekking minimum 15 years; chimpanzee tracking minimum 12 years.

Fitness Requirements: Both treks require moderate fitness. Gorilla trekking involves hiking at altitude (2,500-3,000m) for 2-6 hours round trip. Chimpanzee tracking involves walking through forest trails, sometimes with steep sections. Porters available for both treks to assist.

Health Considerations: While gorilla trekking areas are malaria-free, Nyungwe Forest is in a malaria zone. Consult doctor about prophylaxis. Yellow fever vaccination certificate required. COVID-19 requirements should be checked prior to travel.

Equipment: Waterproof hiking boots essential for both treks. Rain jacket, long pants, long-sleeved shirts, gardening gloves (for gorilla trekking against nettles). Camera with zoom lens recommended.

Safari Highlights:

  • Mountain Gorilla Encounter: One-hour with habituated gorilla families in Volcanoes National Park
  • Chimpanzee Tracking: One-hour observation of chimpanzee troops in Nyungwe Forest
  • Nyungwe Canopy Walk: East Africa's only canopy walkway through forest treetops
  • Golden Monkey Tracking: Optional tracking of these rare, colorful primates
  • Colobus Monkey Troops: See large groups of black-and-white colobus monkeys
  • Scenic Landscapes: Dramatic volcanic views and ancient rainforest scenery
  • Bird Watching: Over 300 bird species in Nyungwe including many endemics
  • Cultural Experiences: Optional tea plantation visits and local community interactions

🌳 Nyungwe Forest Canopy Walk Experience

Unique Feature: Nyungwe Forest National Park is home to East Africa's only canopy walkway, offering a truly unique perspective of the rainforest ecosystem. The walkway consists of a series of suspension bridges 50 meters above the forest floor, spanning 160 meters across a steep valley.

Experience Highlights:

  • Elevated Perspective: View the forest from treetop level, normally inaccessible to visitors
  • Wildlife Viewing: Spot canopy-dwelling birds, monkeys, and other wildlife from unique angle
  • Educational Value: Learn about canopy ecology, epiphytes, and forest stratification
  • Photographic Opportunities: Spectacular photos of forest canopy and scenic views
  • Accessibility: Suitable for most visitors with reasonable fitness (some steps involved)

Safety: The canopy walkway is engineered to high safety standards with harnesses provided. Guides accompany all visitors. Not recommended for those with severe fear of heights.

About the National Parks & Primates

Volcanoes National Park: Located in northwestern Rwanda, this park protects the Rwandan portion of the Virunga Mountains, home to approximately one-third of the world's remaining mountain gorillas. The park covers 160 km² of montane rainforest and bamboo forest, with altitudes ranging from 2,400 to 4,507 meters. In addition to gorillas, it hosts golden monkeys, forest elephants, buffalo, and numerous bird species.

Nyungwe Forest National Park: One of Africa's oldest and best-preserved rainforests, covering 1,019 km² in southwestern Rwanda. The forest is estimated to be over 10,000 years old and hosts incredible biodiversity: 13 primate species (including chimpanzees and colobus monkeys), 300+ bird species, 1,000+ plant species, and 120+ butterfly species. Nyungwe is a critical water catchment area feeding both the Nile and Congo river systems.

Mountain Gorillas: Endangered primates with only about 1,000 individuals remaining in the wild. They live in stable family groups led by a dominant silverback. Gorilla tourism has been crucial to their conservation, providing funding for protection and involving local communities.

Chimpanzees: Our closest living relatives, sharing about 98% of our DNA. They are highly intelligent, using tools and exhibiting complex social behaviors. Nyungwe's chimpanzees are part of the eastern chimpanzee subspecies.

Conservation Success: Both parks represent conservation success stories. Mountain gorilla numbers have increased from around 250 in the 1980s to over 1,000 today. Nyungwe's protection has preserved one of Africa's most important biodiversity hotspots.

Primate Species You May Encounter

Volcanoes National Park:

  • Mountain Gorillas: Approximately 10 habituated family groups available for tracking
  • Golden Monkeys: Endangered primates unique to the Virunga region with striking golden-orange fur
  • Other primates: Occasional sightings of blue monkeys and black-and-white colobus

Nyungwe Forest National Park:

  • Chimpanzees: Several habituated communities available for tracking
  • Angolan Colobus Monkeys: Massive troops of up to 400 individuals - spectacular to observe
  • L'Hoest's Monkeys: Beautiful monkeys with distinctive white beard and chest
  • Other primates: Olive baboons, vervet monkeys, grey-cheeked mangabeys, red-tailed monkeys, blue monkeys, dwarf galagos, pottos
  • Total: 13 primate species - the highest primate diversity in East Africa

Best Viewing Times: Early morning for both gorillas and chimpanzees when they are most active. Golden monkeys are active throughout the day. Colobus monkeys can be seen at various times.

Practical Information for Your Primate Safari

Best Time to Visit: Rwanda can be visited year-round, but optimal conditions are during dry seasons:

  • June to September: Long dry season - best for gorilla trekking (less muddy) and forest walking
  • December to February: Short dry season - also excellent conditions
  • March to May & October to November: Rainy seasons - landscapes greener, fewer tourists, but trekking can be more challenging with muddy trails

What to Pack:

  • Trekking Gear: Waterproof hiking boots, rain jacket, gardening gloves (for gorilla trekking), long pants, long-sleeved shirts
  • Forest Clothing: Neutral colors for chimpanzee tracking, light layers for changing temperatures
  • Equipment: Binoculars, camera with zoom lens, spare batteries, memory cards
  • Health: Personal medication, insect repellent, sunscreen, hand sanitizer, malaria prophylaxis for Nyungwe
  • Documents: Passport, visa, primate permit copies, insurance documents, vaccination certificates

Transportation: Comfortable 4x4 vehicles for transfers between destinations. Professional drivers/guides with knowledge of parks and primates.

Physical Requirements: Both gorilla and chimpanzee tracking require moderate fitness. Gorilla trekking involves 2-6 hours of hiking at altitudes of 2,500-3,000m over steep, sometimes muddy terrain. Chimpanzee tracking involves walking on forest trails, sometimes with steep sections. Porters available for hire to assist with carrying bags.

Essential Primate Safari Tips

  • Permit Planning: Gorilla permits must be booked 6-9 months in advance for peak season. Chimpanzee permits 3 months in advance.
  • Fitness Preparation: Moderate fitness required for both treks. Consider pre-trip walking/hiking to prepare.
  • Altitude Acclimatization: Spend first night at altitude before gorilla trekking to adjust.
  • Porter Hire: Recommended for both treks ($15-20) - supports local community and makes trekking easier.
  • Primate Rules: Maintain appropriate distances (7m for gorillas), no flash photography, follow guide instructions carefully.
  • Health Precautions: Malaria prophylaxis for Nyungwe, yellow fever vaccination required, check current COVID-19 requirements.
  • Packing Strategy: Waterproof everything - plastic bags for electronics essential. Break in hiking boots before trip.
  • Cash: Bring USD for tips, souvenirs, and additional activities. Small bills recommended.
  • Flexibility: Primate viewing depends on animal movements - patience enhances experience.
  • Responsible Tourism: Follow all guidelines to minimize impact on primates and their habitat.
