African Safari in Tanzania: Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

July 28, 2026 14 min read Tanzania

Tanzania is one of Africa's most spectacular safari destinations, offering an unmatched combination of abundant wildlife, breathtaking landscapes, rich cultural heritage, and world-renowned national parks. From witnessing millions of wildebeest thunder across the Serengeti during the Great Migration to spotting tree-climbing lions in Lake Manyara and descending into the wildlife-rich Ngorongoro Crater, Tanzania delivers unforgettable safari experiences for every type of traveler.

Home to some of the largest protected wildlife areas on the continent, Tanzania is a dream destination for photographers, honeymooners, families, adventure seekers, and first-time safari visitors alike. Whether you're planning a luxury fly-in safari, a budget camping adventure, or combining your wildlife experience with a relaxing beach holiday in Zanzibar, Tanzania offers endless possibilities.

Why Choose Tanzania for an African Safari?

Tanzania is home to more than 20 national parks, numerous game reserves, and conservation areas that protect some of the richest wildlife populations on Earth. It is one of the few countries where you can witness the Big Five — lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo, and rhinoceros — alongside the spectacular Great Wildebeest Migration.

Beyond wildlife, Tanzania boasts incredible natural diversity, from the endless plains of the Serengeti and the volcanic beauty of the Ngorongoro Crater to Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa's tallest mountain, and the white-sand beaches of Zanzibar.

Best Safari Parks in Tanzania

Serengeti National Park

World-famous for the Great Wildebeest Migration, home to lions, leopards, cheetahs, and elephants. Hot air balloon safaris available.

Ngorongoro Conservation Area

World's largest intact volcanic caldera, high concentration of wildlife including black rhinos, lions, elephants, and flamingos.

Tarangire National Park

Famous for ancient baobab trees, enormous elephant herds, and over 550 bird species. Excellent during the dry season.

Lake Manyara National Park

Known for tree-climbing lions, large baboon troops, flamingos, and diverse ecosystems including groundwater forests and hot springs.

Nyerere National Park

Africa's largest national park, home to elephants, African wild dogs, crocodiles, hippos, and boat safaris along the Rufiji River.

Ruaha National Park

Tanzania's largest park, known for large lion prides, leopards, elephants, kudu, sable antelope, and exceptional birdlife.

The Great Wildebeest Migration

The Great Migration is one of the world's greatest wildlife spectacles and the highlight of any safari in Tanzania. Every year, more than two million wildebeest, accompanied by hundreds of thousands of zebras and Thomson's gazelles, undertake a remarkable circular journey across the Serengeti ecosystem in search of fresh grazing and water.

December – March

Calving season in Southern Serengeti & Ndutu Plains. Nearly half a million calves are born, attracting large predators.

April – June

Herds move northwest through Central and Western Serengeti, crossing rivers and open plains in search of fresh pasture.

July – October

Dramatic Mara River crossings in Northern Serengeti. Thousands of animals brave crocodile-filled waters in Africa's most iconic wildlife spectacle.

November

Migration moves south as herds return to the Serengeti's southern plains, completing the annual cycle.

Wildlife You Can Expect to See

Tanzania is home to some of Africa's most diverse and abundant wildlife, making it one of the world's premier safari destinations.

The Big Five

Lion
Leopard
African Elephant
Cape Buffalo
Black Rhinoceros

Predators

  • Cheetahs — World's fastest land animals, commonly seen on the open plains of the Serengeti.
  • Spotted Hyenas — Highly intelligent hunters and scavengers that often compete with lions for food.
  • African Wild Dogs — One of Africa's rarest carnivores, occasionally seen in Nyerere and Ruaha National Parks.

Herbivores

Common species include wildebeests, plains zebras, Masai giraffes, hippos, warthogs, elands, waterbucks, topis, hartebeests, impalas, Grant's gazelles, Thomson's gazelles, dik-diks, bushbucks, reedbucks, and kudus.

Birdlife

Tanzania is a paradise for birdwatchers, with more than 1,100 recorded bird species. Popular birds include African Fish Eagle, Secretary Bird, Kori Bustard, Lilac-breasted Roller, Grey Crowned Crane, Saddle-billed Stork, flamingos, ostriches, hornbills, bee-eaters, kingfishers, sunbirds, and vultures.

Best Time to Visit Tanzania

  • June to October: Dry season — best for wildlife viewing as animals gather around rivers and waterholes.
  • December to March: Excellent wildlife viewing in Southern Serengeti during wildebeest calving season.
  • November & April to May: Greener months with fewer tourists, excellent birdwatching, and lower accommodation rates.

What to Pack

  • Lightweight, neutral-colored clothing
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Wide-brimmed hat and sunglasses
  • Sunscreen and insect repellent
  • Camera and binoculars
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Rain jacket (during the wet season)
  • Personal medications

Why Combine Tanzania with Zanzibar?

After exciting days on safari, Zanzibar offers the perfect place to relax. Its white-sand beaches, turquoise waters, coral reefs, spice farms, and historic Stone Town create an ideal ending to an African adventure.

Popular activities include snorkeling, scuba diving, dolphin tours, spice tours, sailing on traditional dhow boats, and simply unwinding along the Indian Ocean.

Final Thoughts

A Tanzania safari is more than a holiday — it's an unforgettable journey into one of the world's greatest wildlife destinations. Whether you're witnessing the Great Migration in the Serengeti, exploring the wildlife-filled Ngorongoro Crater, photographing elephants beneath towering baobab trees in Tarangire, or relaxing on Zanzibar's pristine beaches, Tanzania offers experiences unlike anywhere else in Africa.

With its extraordinary wildlife, diverse landscapes, welcoming people, and world-class safari experiences, Tanzania remains one of the ultimate destinations for anyone dreaming of an authentic African safari.
