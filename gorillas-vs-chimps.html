Gorillas vs. Chimpanzees: Key Differences for Trekkers in Uganda and Rwanda

July 30, 2026 12 min read Primate Trekking

Uganda and Rwanda are two of Africa's best destinations for unforgettable primate encounters, offering travelers the rare opportunity to trek through ancient forests in search of mountain gorillas and chimpanzees. While both species are great apes and share many similarities with humans, they are remarkably different in appearance, behaviour, habitat, and trekking experience.

For travelers planning a primate safari in East Africa, understanding the differences between gorilla trekking and chimpanzee trekking helps you choose the right experience or plan an itinerary that combines both adventures.

Overview: Gorillas and Chimpanzees as Great Apes

Gorillas and chimpanzees belong to the family of great apes, which also includes humans, orangutans, and bonobos. Unlike monkeys, great apes do not have tails and are known for their high intelligence, complex social structures, and human-like behaviors.

However, millions of years of evolution have shaped gorillas and chimpanzees into very different animals. Gorillas are known for their strength, calm nature, and peaceful lifestyle, while chimpanzees are energetic, highly social, and constantly active.

Quick Comparison: Gorillas vs Chimpanzees

Feature Mountain Gorillas Chimpanzees
Size Large, up to 150kg+ Smaller, 40-70kg
Body Type Muscular, broad chest Slim, agile build
Social Structure Stable family groups (5-20+ members) Fission-fusion communities (30-100+ members)
Leader Silverback male Alpha male with shifting alliances
Diet Primarily herbivorous Omnivorous (fruit, insects, small mammals)
Behavior Calm, gentle, slow-moving Energetic, curious, active
Habitat High-altitude montane forests Tropical and lowland rainforests
Trekking Experience Peaceful, intimate, emotional Energetic, fast-paced, exciting

1. Physical Appearance: Size and Body Structure

One of the easiest ways to distinguish gorillas from chimpanzees is their size and physical appearance.

Mountain Gorillas

  • Large muscular bodies
  • Broad chests and shoulders
  • Long arms with powerful hands
  • Dark, thick fur adapted to high-altitude forests
  • Silverback males weigh over 150kg

Chimpanzees

  • Smaller, lightweight bodies
  • Slimmer and more agile build
  • Long arms for climbing and swinging
  • Dark hair covering most of the body
  • Expressive faces with mobile lips and ears

2. Social Structure and Family Life

Gorilla Groups

Mountain gorillas live in stable family groups, often referred to as troops, which are typically led by a dominant adult male known as the silverback. The silverback is the leader, protector, and decision-maker of the family, responsible for guiding the group to feeding areas, choosing nesting sites, resolving conflicts, and defending the family from potential threats.

A Typical Gorilla Family

  • One dominant silverback
  • Several adult females
  • Infant and juvenile gorillas
  • Adolescent gorillas
  • Sometimes one or more subordinate blackback males

Chimpanzee Communities

Chimpanzees live in large and highly complex communities that can include 30 to more than 100 individuals. Unlike mountain gorillas, they do not stay together in one permanent group. Instead, they follow a fission-fusion social system, where members split into smaller temporary groups, known as parties, and reunite throughout the day.

During a chimpanzee trek, visitors may witness fascinating behaviors such as:

  • Complex communication through calls and gestures
  • Social grooming and strong group interactions
  • Cooperative hunting and food sharing
  • Tool use and problem-solving
  • Playful and energetic movements through the forest

3. Habitat: Where to Find Gorillas and Chimpanzees

Gorilla Habitats

Uganda

  • Bwindi Impenetrable National Park
  • Mgahinga Gorilla National Park

Rwanda

  • Volcanoes National Park

High-altitude montane forests, dense rainforest, bamboo forests, mist-covered hills.

Chimpanzee Habitats

Uganda

  • Kibale Forest National Park
  • Budongo Forest (Murchison Falls NP)
  • Kyambura Gorge (Queen Elizabeth NP)

Rwanda

  • Nyungwe Forest National Park

Tropical lowland forests, dense rainforests, tall trees for canopy movement.

4. Trekking Experience: Gorilla Trekking vs Chimpanzee Trekking

Gorilla Trekking

  • Peaceful and intimate encounter
  • Spend up to 1 hour with gorillas
  • Gorillas are calm and relaxed
  • Observe family interactions
  • Deeply emotional experience
  • Often the highlight of the safari

Chimpanzee Trekking

  • Energetic and fast-paced
  • Follow active chimpanzees through forest
  • Watch swinging, climbing, and socializing
  • Hear loud calls and vocalizations
  • Exciting and dynamic experience
  • Witness tool use and intelligence

5. Behavior: Calm Giants vs Active Forest Dwellers

Gorillas: Gentle Forest Giants

Mountain gorillas are peaceful, primarily herbivorous primates that lead a calm and structured lifestyle. They spend much of their day feeding on leaves, stems, bamboo shoots, fruits, and other vegetation before resting and socializing with members of their family group.

During a gorilla trek, visitors may observe gorillas:

  • Feeding on leaves, fruits, bamboo shoots, and other vegetation
  • Resting quietly in the shade of the forest
  • Moving slowly through dense vegetation
  • Mothers caring for and nursing their infants
  • Young gorillas playing under the watchful eye of the silverback

Chimpanzees: Intelligent and Energetic

Chimpanzees are highly intelligent, curious, and energetic primates that are constantly active throughout the day. They spend much of their time moving through the forest canopy in search of food, communicating with one another, grooming, and exploring their surroundings.

During a chimpanzee trek, visitors may see chimpanzees:

  • Communicating through loud calls, gestures, and facial expressions
  • Searching for fruits, insects, and other food sources
  • Socializing and grooming other members of the community
  • Climbing, swinging, and running through the trees
  • Exploring and interacting with their forest environment

6. Diet Differences

Gorillas

  • Primarily herbivorous
  • Leaves, shoots, bamboo, roots, stems
  • Wild celery and seasonal fruits
  • Several kilograms of vegetation daily

Chimpanzees

  • Omnivorous and adaptable
  • Fruits, seeds, leaves, flowers
  • Insects (termites, ants)
  • Occasionally small mammals
  • Use tools to extract food

7. Conservation Status and Importance of Trekking

Both mountain gorillas and chimpanzees face ongoing conservation challenges, including habitat destruction, disease transmission, illegal hunting, and increasing human-wildlife conflict. Fortunately, responsible tourism has become one of the most effective ways of supporting the long-term survival of these remarkable primates.

Conservation Through Tourism

  • Revenue from trekking permits funds conservation efforts
  • Supports ranger patrols and habitat protection
  • Funds gorilla and chimpanzee monitoring
  • Provides veterinary care
  • Supports community development programs
  • Creates employment for local communities

8. Can You Combine Gorilla and Chimpanzee Trekking?

Yes. Many travelers choose to combine gorilla trekking and chimpanzee trekking into one unforgettable primate safari, allowing them to experience the unique behaviors and habitats of both species during a single trip.

A typical combined primate safari may include:

  • Kibale Forest National Park for chimpanzee trekking
  • Bwindi Impenetrable National Park for mountain gorilla trekking
  • Volcanoes National Park in Rwanda for another gorilla trekking experience
  • Nyungwe Forest National Park for chimpanzee trekking and rainforest exploration

Conclusion: Which Experience Is Better?

There is no definitive answer because gorilla trekking and chimpanzee trekking offer two completely different but equally rewarding wildlife experiences. Gorilla trekking provides a peaceful and emotional encounter with gentle giants living in mist-covered mountain forests, while chimpanzee trekking offers a lively and energetic adventure following intelligent primates through tropical rainforests.

If you dream of a quiet and intimate wildlife encounter, gorilla trekking is the perfect choice. If you enjoy observing active animals, fascinating social behavior, and constant movement through the forest, chimpanzee trekking is equally exciting.

For travelers seeking the ultimate primate safari, combining both experiences in Uganda and Rwanda offers the best of both worlds. Together, mountain gorillas and chimpanzees provide an extraordinary insight into the lives of our closest living relatives while directly supporting conservation efforts that help protect these endangered species and their forest habitats for generations to come.
